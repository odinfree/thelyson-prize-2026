## Setting and desires

Merewick is an invented English Midlands river town. The story runs from Saturday 16 May 2026 at 07:00 to Sunday 17 May at 07:00, in British Summer Time. Buildings, travel distances, businesses and local river arrangements are invented. [Setting research](../../research/notes/2026-10-04-setting-logistics.md) supplies limited physical references and approximate daylight.

Flora died at 41. Leo was 44 then and is 59 now. Jo was 15 and turns 30 today. Flora wants to make a new shared experience with each of them, while also wanting to remain the person who knows them best. Jo wants dinner in the home she has made functional, with friends who know her adult self. She works in building conservation, producing surveys and drawings; she is not presented as a structural engineer. Leo wants to share a skill acquired after Flora's death. He sold the former family house, lives alone in a cottage and had a six-year relationship with Catherine that ended two years ago. Catherine kept her own house. Bess is his older rowing friend.

Ordinary bodies eat, tire, sleep and feel pain. Flora remembers her life before death; the fifteen intervening years do not arrive as hidden knowledge. She arranged the return while ill. The mechanism and any afterlife remain outside her knowledge. One return runs from seven to seven, with collection wherever she is. There is no extension, repeat, magical rescue or retrospective account of the survivors after she leaves.

Jo rents a first-floor flat near Harper Street. The table has a 118 cm round top and removable legs. The entrance has 74 cm clear width; carrying it upright still requires space at the turn, a cleared route and room for the carriers. The warehouse's old underside split and Flora's later new edge chip are separate defects. No active woodworm is established. The six planned diners are Jo, Arun, Nina, Mara, Phil and Ellen, Flora's older sister. Patrick is the possible seventh; Flora's later invitation makes eight when he comes. The folding table supplies additional places. Leo chooses supper at home and an evening walk with Flora. Arun is a friend who works in a restaurant; no romance is implied.

## Object and knowledge handoffs

The six photographs are fixed: Leo with Bess's borrowed paddle in the skiff; Jo holding decorated plaster at work; Leo and Catherine on a wet coastal walk; Jo's first supper in the current flat; boat-club cleanup after a winter flood; and Leo's poor tomato crop with Jo's label. The envelope stays with Flora in the green dress through chapter 7. In chapter 8 she puts the folded dress and original shoes into the blue bag, and the envelope into its inner zipped pocket. Later chapters must enact any further transfer.

The photographed paddle is not one of the rowing oars. An early loose rowlock required paddles for returning; Leo dropped his at a stop and borrowed Bess's. The present outing uses a sound boat and two checked oars. The old leak has been repaired. Research puts approximate sunrise near 05:10 on Sunday 17 May; the outing begins later, subject to actual visibility and calm fictional conditions.

The phone belongs to Leo. Flora uses it only through an enacted loan or handoff. Keys remain with their owners except explicit transfers. The new table chip stays in an opened bill marked TABLE; smoothing the exposed edge is not an invisible repair of the whole table. Seats, serving dish, clothes and transport are tracked in the chapter handoffs and checked against prose.

## Development decisions and revision boundary

The [warehouse](../../experiments/manuscript-development/warehouse/draft-01.md) is adopted without padding, with one incidental sock reference repaired to ankle. Moving the chapter's arrival after nine reconciles Arun's remark. The original candidate is retained.

The [middle pilot](../../experiments/manuscript-development/middle/draft-01.md) establishes practical work and bodily appetite. Its first house-sale disclosure, green dress, Bess's lunch departure and unclear one-oar demonstration cannot be imported into chapter 9 unchanged. The integrated chapter must account for already known facts, changed clothing, afternoon timing and two-oar technique. The [development review](../reviews/2026-10-04-development-review.md) records these issues against exact versions.

The [two ending alternatives](../../experiments/manuscript-development/middle/endings.md) remain available for structural comparison. Ending B, walk and dawn row, is selected because the actual middle creates a second desired experience. Its cost is leaving Jo despite wanting to stay. Ending A, remaining at the flat, would require acknowledging the forfeited outing and Leo's disappointment. Neither is supported by measured reader preference.
