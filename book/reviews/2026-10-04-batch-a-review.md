## Structural and voice recommendation

The early arc produces consequences beyond symbolic lessons: Jo purchases a table, Flora damages it while attempting useful work, Jo handles an expensive professional mistake, groceries get bought with Jo's money, seats are arranged and invitations made, and Leo's house sale is disclosed through an actual visit. These actions support continuing into the afternoon. No major new early chapter or replacement plot is required by this review.

There is, however, a repeated habit of explaining an emotional implication immediately after the action has made it legible. It can make Flora sound more continuously self-diagnosing than her repeated mistakes warrant. Two bounded revision candidates are:

- Chapter 4's cake-tin paragraph after the abrupt “No”: the imagined keepsakes, actual cables, and Jo closing the lid already establish the disappointment. Consider shortening the subsequent explanation beginning “There was no reason for it to contain an account of me.” Keep the physical tin, her unwanted hope and return to the drawings.
- Chapter 6's new-library exchange: the stopped sentence and Flora making Jo finish it already carry the time limit into ordinary conversation. Consider shortening the explanatory sentence beginning “I have wanted her to speak without thinking about how long I have.” Keep Jo's unplanned recommendation and the pickle action rather than replacing the passage with a maxim.

These are scene-specific editorial recommendations, not universal bans on reflection. Preserve self-exposure when it changes the reading, such as Flora wanting mild woodworm to delay Jo, or wanting an inconvenient old hedge back. The narrator should still be capable of noticing her own ugliness; she need not explain every instance for the reader.
