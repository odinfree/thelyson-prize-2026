## Opening and warehouse findings

**The warehouse candidate earns its next scene.** Jo actually negotiates the price, pays, receives a receipt, dismantles the table with the others and invites Flora to help at the flat. The invitation does not silently become an invitation to dinner. The photograph envelope remains in the dress pocket from Chapter 1; the blue clothes bag is put down and explicitly collected. The bag of bolts and washers becomes Flora's carried responsibility. No return rule changes.

The reunion is physically and emotionally particular: Jo first addresses the accidental chair movement, then Flora's first remark lands on a spot rather than the reunion she rehearsed. Their overlapping apologies and caught zip delay separation without assigning Jo an unspoken motive. Flora's misjudgments remain hers. Her wish for woodworm in Chapter 1 is answered through Jo's qualified inspection, not a punishment or declaration that Flora has learned a lesson.

Jo and Leo have effective agency on the page. Jo chooses the table, sets the bargain and checks the hardware. Leo retrieves a washer and teaches Flora a task while declining to speak over Jo's negotiation. Flora is useful and funny as well as possessive. The scene contains a high density of quips, but several are attached to distinct bodies and practical difficulties; a general joke-removal pass would flatten its voice.

No prose-level blocking repair is recommended for this candidate. Its 1,833 words meet the original 1,500–2,000 development brief. Do not add words merely to reach the later outline's 1,900–2,200 guide.

## Bounded continuity decisions before dependent drafting

1. **Fix the outline clock, not the warehouse prose.** The inspected outline schedules arrival at 08:50, but Arun says Jo has been there since nine. Chapter 1 supplies no exact café departure time. The integrator's proposed café end around 09:05, warehouse 09:15–09:50, and delivery from 10:00 is sufficient; reconcile later travel intervals at the same time. This is an outline/prose mismatch, not an established error within the manuscript.
2. **Freeze the boat's propulsion equipment.** Chapter 1 explicitly calls the photographed object a paddle; Bess lent it and Leo's own is at the bottom of the river. The outline later proposes rowing and ashore rigging. Both can coexist, but a later scene needs an ordinary explanation of why that photograph involved a paddle and what equipment the actual outing uses. Do not silently rename the established paddle an oar or alternate strokes, fittings and craft types.
3. **Retain two distinct table defects.** The warehouse has an old underside split and old surface marks. The carrying chapter plans a new edge chip. Subsequent modest work should say what it does; it cannot silently restore every defect. Jo's purchase is already enacted despite the old split.
4. **Treat Catherine's photograph as selected disclosure.** It is planned as one of the six images Leo deliberately brought. Flora can encounter it unexpectedly, but Leo's choice to include it should remain intelligible. An accidental-secret plot would contradict the more interesting prior act of selection unless the text supplies a separate reason.

These observations were sent to the integrator during drafting so dependent chapters need not wait for this document.

## Whole-book risks to inspect in actual chapters

- **Repeated emotional mechanism.** The full outline contains several opportunities for Flora to presume, be corrected, then privately recognize her presumption: table, stored object, house, Catherine, recipe, public anecdote and departure. The warehouse itself varies this with a consequential purchase and shared work. Later chapters should preserve pleasures and actions that are not promptly converted into instruction. This requires scene choices, not a forbidden-word list.
- **Other people's lives must produce action.** The planned work, later relationship, birthday friends and boat practice can give Jo and Leo independent lives, but labels alone do not. Preserve Leo's actual evening preference, Jo's choices about dinner and her dawn arrival, and an ordinary disagreement between them Flora cannot solve. Do not use unanimous guest disapproval as a jury against Flora.
- **The middle needs appetite before the evening choice.** If Flora leaves the party merely because the outline needs her beside Leo, the departure will feel mechanical. The boat-shed work and reciprocal attraction must establish something she actively wants. Jo's disappointment can coexist with that desire and need not be secretly relief.
- **The late sequence risks several successive endings.** Photographs, a night message, a dawn row, cleanup and collection each invite a closing speech. Give each a practical development and reserve irreversible departure for the actual boundary. A note should earn its place instead of repeating the night call or final scene.
- **Length remains unproven.** Twenty-two activities and word guides are a drafting hypothesis. Existing prose supports the opening and warehouse; it does not demonstrate that each later errand sustains 2,000 words or that a full novel works. Whole-book review should cut repetition or change structure rather than fill guides with retrospective explanation.

## Middle and endings

**The middle succeeds as a development test but needs integration work before becoming Chapter 9.** The leather repair begins, encounters a difficulty, is completed and inspected; Leo then attends to the other oar. Flora moves from offering help to wanting the physical action of rowing, after feeling its shape in the boat. Her expressed wish to return to Jo remains alongside her pleasure with Leo. Bess has her own paintwork and next appointment, and moves away without delivering an interpretation of the couple's relationship. The scene's unease does not extinguish the practical desire it has made possible.

Four specific integration issues remain:

1. The candidate first reveals the house sale and cottage, while the outline already reveals these in Chapters 7–8. If kept in Chapter 9, the scene needs a new, consequential point of friction; it cannot repeat the same first discovery. Preserve the interrupted joint task and subsequent mutual help, but let the new information actually be new. Alternatively, relocating the entire scene would require explicit outline changes and later timing checks.
2. Flora still wears the dress here, including a skirt adjustment and wiping with its hem, while Chapter 8 plans the trousers and shirt change. Revise these concrete actions when integrating; changing only one garment noun would leave the second contradiction.
3. Bess leaves for her sister's lunch, although the outline places the scene around 15:20–16:15. A later meal/visit is a small sufficient change if that clock is retained. Do not silently move the whole afternoon earlier.
4. Check the rowing demonstration as a single mechanical sequence. The text introduces two oars but puts both Flora's hands around the handle of one and then moves them apart. This may be intended as an isolated demonstration, but it is unclear preparation for working a pair of oars. Establish the boat's arrangement and demonstrate the corresponding grip. This review has not technically certified the boat or repair.

The middle brief already permits a rowing skiff with an emergency paddle in the photograph; the actual candidate has not yet enacted that explanation. The future boating chapter must also assess present conditions rather than treating the repaired sleeve as a full safety certificate.

**Ending B is a reasonable working choice.** The actual middle supplies bodily interest in rowing, so the proposed outing is less arbitrary than it was in the abstract outline. This finding does not yet establish the party departure: desire for a night walk and the cost of leaving Jo must still appear in those chapters. Ending A remains plausible but would require acknowledging the outing Flora forfeits. Neither alternative warrants a claim of greater reader appeal from this evidence.
