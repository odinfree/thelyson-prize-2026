## Repairs that matter

**B-01 — Resolve the incompatible memory states across chapters 14 and 15.** In [chapter 14](../chapters/14-the-story-i-tell.md), lines 163–167, Flora recovers Jo's exact request and asserts “I had promised.” In [chapter 15](../chapters/15-after-the-plates.md), lines 37–39, she explicitly cannot remember the promise and tells Jo so. This changes the truth of her apology; it is more than a phrasing preference. Recommended disposition, agreed by the integrator: retain chapter 14's remembered pre-concert anxiety and Flora's advice to ask for the lower key, but remove its recovered after-concert promise and retrospective certainty. Preserve the public correction of the wrong key and invented stamp. Chapter 15 can then keep its more difficult act of believing Jo without recovering the missing memory. Re-read both the public correction and private apology after repair; do not merely delete the word “promised.”

**B-02 — Give the boat scene a different movement from the disclosures around it.** [Chapter 9](../chapters/09-the-seam.md), lines 141–185, turns the news of Jo's earlier outings into another threat to Flora's special place, including imagined accusation and apology. It follows Catherine's substantial disclosure in chapter 8 and precedes the Jo/Ellen private-conversation exchange in chapter 10. The cumulative effect risks making successive activities deliver the same correction. Keep Jo's prior visits as necessary knowledge for her later familiarity with the boat. Compress Flora's reaction to one mixed feeling, then let curiosity, the crossed stitch and shared repair resume. Preserve the completed repair, reciprocal assistance and bodily desire produced by holding the oars. This is a structural recommendation from this sequence, not a ban on jealousy or disagreement.
