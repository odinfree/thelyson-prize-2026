## The novel

Flora returns bodily fifteen years after her death for one day, chosen to coincide with Jo's thirtieth birthday. Leo meets her. Flora wants familiar intimacy and a new experience with each of them, while also wanting to remain the person who knows them best. Jo wants a good dinner in the home she has made functional. Leo wants to share a skill and a present life built after Flora's death.

The day moves through a table purchase and damaged delivery, Jo's work and birthday preparations, Leo's former and present homes, an oar repair, dinner, a confidence mishandled in public, an evening walk and a short dawn row. The people enjoy one another, make mistakes and finish particular jobs without settling every claim on their time. Jo chooses to come to the landing; collection remains irreversible.

The intended experience is an intimate family novel with bodily immediacy, dry humor and emotional pressure carried by ordinary actions. This is a craft intention and AI editorial assessment, not measured reader response or imitation of a named writer.

## Constraints for any further revision

- A practical scene must change circumstances even when nobody learns a lesson. Preserve mutual enjoyment, discovery, work, disagreement and solitude as different activities.
- Keep Flora loving, perceptive, possessive and fallible. Jo and Leo have aims beyond correcting her or accommodating the return.
- Preserve meaningful repetition, ambiguity and rhythm. Do not turn the review's local commentary cuts into universal word or sentence bans.
- Separate remembered experience, others' testimony and inference. Flora can believe Jo about a promise she cannot herself recover.
- Keep the single return boundary. No new magic, dangerous rescue or retrospective summary of the survivors should supply an easier ending.
