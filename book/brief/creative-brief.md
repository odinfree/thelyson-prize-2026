Updated 4 October 2026. **The user selected this concept.** Selection does not approve every earlier outline idea or make the working title final. This brief records development direction, not manuscript prose.

## The book we are developing

Flora returns bodily fifteen years after her death for one day, chosen to coincide with Jo's thirtieth birthday. Leo comes to meet her. Flora wants the familiar intimacy of her family, but both have lives and purposes that continued without her. The story follows what these three people actually choose to do with the day, including pleasure, irritation and mistakes that do not resolve neatly.

The proposed reading experience is an intimate family novel with one speculative premise: bodily immediacy, dry humor and emotional pressure carried by ordinary actions. Flora can be loving, funny, perceptive and possessive at once. Jo and Leo need desires beyond accommodating her or teaching her to let go. This is a craft direction inherited from the selected opening, not a measured reader response or imitation of a named author.

The next scene is the warehouse reunion with adult Jo and the table decision. Later invitations, a birthday gathering attended by Flora, a walk and a boat outing remain hypotheses. An evening party and morning boat trip do not inherently conflict. If Flora chooses one desired experience over another, establish her attraction to both through action.

Before the full outline, decide the present-day desire Flora pursues beyond spending time together; what Jo wants from this birthday; what Leo wants from this encounter; enough setting and travel detail to make choices credible; and only the return rules that later scenes require. Exact adult ages beyond Jo's thirty appear in earlier planning but are not all stated in the prose; keep that distinction in the [story bible](../continuity/story-bible.md).

## Development tests

- A practical scene should change someone's circumstances even if Flora learns no lesson.
- Vary the action: mutual enjoyment, shared work, discovery, disagreement, solitude and choice. Avoid making every scene a request followed by a correction.
- A remembered event earns space when it changes a present action or interpretation. Do not pad the day with explanatory flashbacks.
- Flora observes, remembers, infers and sometimes guesses. She cannot narrate others' unexpressed motives as facts.
- The final encounter must keep the return boundary while allowing relationships to remain mixed or unfinished. A magical exception cannot supply the payoff.
