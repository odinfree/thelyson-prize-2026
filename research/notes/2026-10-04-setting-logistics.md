## Table: what the dimensions establish

A 118 cm circular tabletop can pass through a 74 cm clear doorway **upright, edge first**, if the detached top's thickness (including any retained apron or fittings), the doorway height, approach and exit space, and the carriers' handling room allow it. Its diameter alone does not make it fit. This is a geometric inference, not a tested delivery. A turn immediately beyond the door can still stop progress. Do not claim that a 74 cm measurement verifies the whole route.

[John Lewis's furniture access guide](https://static.johnlewiscontent.com/v1/static/Will-it-Fit-Guide-John-Lewis) asks customers to consider the whole route, dimensions of delivered components, doorway height and width, hallways, corners, stairs and obstructions. It also notes that components can be rotated. Although its boxed-product framing differs from an unboxed secondhand table, the access distinctions apply to the fictional problem.

**Useful fictional decisions, to adopt explicitly rather than attribute to a source:** the table has four removable legs; its remaining top/apron is much thinner than 74 cm; the door is taller than 118 cm; the turn opens into enough clear landing or hall space to pivot the top; an obstructing shoe rack or piece of furniture can be moved. Describe a measured attempt with somebody watching the wall and fingers, not an automatic consequence of roundness. Retain bolts/washers with the legs. Removing the legs is not necessarily the same operation as removing the whole underframe. If a later scene needs a failed first attempt, make the obstruction specific and reversible instead of changing the established door width.

The source café scene says only “seventy-four,” with no units. Centimetres is a **new setting choice**, not a fact recovered from C01. C01 also contains a possible hole, not a verified active woodworm infestation. No pest diagnosis was researched or established here.

## Date and daylight

Python's Gregorian `datetime.date` confirms **16 May 2026 is Saturday**, and **17 May is Sunday**. The final morning therefore uses the latter date. All story clocks below use **BST / UTC+1**, selected explicitly for this mid-May English setting.

A local calculation used [NOAA's published approximate solar equations](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/solareqns.PDF), with illustrative coordinates **52.4862° N, 1.8904° W**, fractional year evaluated at noon, and zenith angles 90.833° for sunrise/sunset and 96° for civil twilight. The [Royal Observatory Greenwich explanation](https://www.rmg.co.uk/stories/space-astronomy/when-dawn) defines civil dawn by the Sun's centre reaching six degrees below the horizon. These are approximate astronomical times for the Birmingham area, not observed weather or a surveyed Merewick horizon.
