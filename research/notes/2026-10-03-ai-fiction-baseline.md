# AI fiction: initial evidence and experiments

Research date: 2026-10-03. Status: first reading pass, not a systematic review. Six primary studies are registered in [literature.json](../sources/literature.json). ArXiv versions below are preprints unless a conference publication is named. No generation experiment has been run.

## Working recommendation

Start with explicit narrative decisions, an evolving continuity record, and blind comparisons of small samples. Treat cleaner sentences, coherent events, distinctiveness, and reader engagement as separate outcomes. These are working hypotheses to test with our chosen model and intended readers, not a proven recipe for a contest novel. None of these studies establishes that its method produces a prizeworthy 40,000–120,000-word book.

## What the evidence supports

**L01 — Planning deserves a baseline.** DOC combines hierarchical outlines with controls that keep generated passages aligned with them. Human comparisons report improvements over Re3 in coherence, relevance, and interestingness. This supports testing outline control, not merely asking for an outline and ignoring it. The study concerns several-thousand-word stories and GPT-3-era systems; its effect sizes are not estimates for current models or novel length. [Yang et al., ACL 2023, abstract and methods](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.190/)

**L02 — Track events and what each chapter must do.** StoryWriter separates event outlining, chapter planning, and writing with compressed relevant history. Its reported stories average 8,081 words, with higher aggregate human ratings than the tested baselines. However, outputs differ substantially in length, and it does not lead every quality dimension. English graduate students and GPT-4o provide different evaluation signals. We can borrow the separation of responsibilities without assuming multiple agents automatically improve fiction. [Xia et al., 2025, sections 2–3 and Table 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.16445v1)

**L03 — Idea assistance can also create convergence.** In a randomized study of 293 writers producing eight-sentence stories, access to GPT-4 ideas improved reader ratings while making assisted stories more similar to one another. These were human-written micro-stories, with limited prompts and no iterative collaboration; this is not evidence that all-AI novels must be homogeneous. It motivates preserving several independently developed directions before selecting one. [Doshi and Hauser, Science Advances 2024, study and limitations](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10195027/1/Generative%20AI%20enhances%20individual%20creativity%20but%20reduces%20the%20collective%20diversity%20of%20novel%20content.pdf)

**L04 — A plausible critic can miss the main defect.** StoryFeedback uses 1,300 short stories with deliberately introduced problems. Tested models often give specific, useful-looking feedback while missing the most consequential issue or misjudging when praise is appropriate. The paper evaluates feedback, not whether repeated revisions yield better novels; errors are synthetic and the tested models are older. Require textual evidence and a ranked diagnosis before accepting a revision. [Rashkin et al., ACL 2025, sections 6–7 and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.16007v1)

**L05 — Reader population changes the result.** A preregistered excerpt study compares MFA-trained writers and AI on passages up to 450 words. The March 2026 version includes 28 MFA-trained and 516 college-educated general readers. Their preferences differ for prompted AI, while author-specific fine-tuning changes preferences in both groups. This is an author-emulation task using older models, not novel evaluation or an argument to imitate an author. Keep craft-trained and intended-reader feedback visible separately; neither represents the contest jury or its markets. [Chakrabarty, Ginsburg, and Dhillon, arXiv v4, methods and results](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13939v4)

**L06 — Revision should specify the intended change and what to preserve.** CraftAlign uses explicit narrative/style features to select revision guidance. It reports benefits over generic and randomly selected guidance; its human study covers 20 eligible story groups with eight reviewers each. The main human outcome is perceived human-likeness, not literary merit, sustained enjoyment, or novel coherence. Feature labels also come from a model-derived pipeline. Borrow its preservation constraints and targeted comparisons as hypotheses; do not make an AI-detector or human-likeness score our objective. [Yang et al., arXiv August 2026, revision evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01377v1)

## Four small experiments to specify before running

All thresholds below are proposed decision rules, not findings. Freeze prompts, model/version, output length, revision allowance, and cost cap before each run. Keep all outputs and negative results. Randomize presentation order and conceal condition names from readers. Pilot samples are too small for strong generalization; disagreement or insufficient errors means inconclusive.

1. **Continuity pilot.** Across three premises, compare a short synopsis against an event outline plus state ledger; generate three connected scenes per condition, matched for length and total model budget. Record contradictions per 1,000 words and blinded engagement ratings. Advance the richer method only if contradictions fall by at least 25% without lower median engagement. Fewer than four baseline contradictions makes the reduction estimate inconclusive.
2. **Critic calibration.** Later create twelve AI-authored fixtures: eight with recorded continuity/causality faults and four controls. Compare unrestricted critique with a ranked, evidence-citing rubric. The rubric must detect at least two additional planted faults without increasing false alarms. Record misses and unsupported criticisms; do not silently rewrite the fixtures during judging.
3. **Revision without voice loss.** On four original AI-generated scenes, compare a generic cleanup request with one diagnosed, targeted intervention, preserving factual events and viewpoint. Obtain blind preferences and record unintended changes. Advance only if targeted revision wins at least three scenes by reader majority, introduces no factual contradiction, and does not reduce voice-distinctiveness ratings. Otherwise retain the original or investigate the failure.
4. **Premise diversity.** Compare twelve independently generated premises from one broad brief with twelve from a precommitted matrix of distinct setting, conflict, and viewpoint constraints. Blind-code repeated central conflicts/endings and rate promise. The matrix must halve duplicate structures without lowering median promise. Inspect structural similarity directly; embeddings alone cannot decide originality.

## Transfer to stop-slop-refined

Candidate lessons are evidence-backed diagnostics, explicit preservation constraints, and separate sentence/scene/book assessments. Promote a rule only after a recorded comparison shows benefit and documents counterexamples. A banned-word count may help find passages to inspect; it is not a literary-quality score. Implementation belongs after these experiments and the repository review, not in this initial research pass.
